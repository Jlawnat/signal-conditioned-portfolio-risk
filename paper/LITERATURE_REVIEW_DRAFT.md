# Literature review draft

Risk-budgeting research separates capital weights from the amount of risk assigned to positions. Roncalli's [2014 treatment](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1889v1) surveys risk parity and budgeting, while Richard and Roncalli's [2019 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05710v1) shows that weight constraints and turnover make constrained risk allocation more complex. The present study uses a simpler stop-gap exposure proxy, so its budgets cannot be read as equal risk contributions, portfolio variance, VaR or expected shortfall.

Dynamic exposure rules have a separate motivation. Moreira and Muir's [2017 journal article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12513) studies volatility-managed factor portfolios. Policy C applies a bounded inverse-volatility rule to a portfolio stop-risk target, with SPY as the volatility reference. This is a different object and data setting; the prior paper motivates a question about scaling rather than predicting a benefit here.

Momentum work provides context for the entry pool. Jegadeesh and Titman [1993](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x) analyze cross-sectional stock winners and losers; Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen [2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.11.003) analyze time-series momentum in futures. MPB combines momentum with pullback/breakout qualification in equities, so these studies do not certify its realized signal quality. Implementation and sample selection can change the apparent opportunity set. Novy-Marx and Velikov [2016](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/29/1/104/1844518) study how trading costs affect anomaly portfolios. Brown and coauthors [1992](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/5/4/553/1590264) demonstrate survivorship-induced distortions in performance research. Bailey and coauthors' [2015 author manuscript](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf) analyzes backtest overfitting. The present use of current S&P 500 constituents and an already inspected window makes these concerns direct limitations, not caveats that can be removed by a bootstrap.

The study's contribution is a transparent comparison of three fixed implementations under two explicitly separated transaction-cap regimes, with both planned risk and realized NAV outcomes reported. It does not claim a new general risk-budgeting theory or an optimized trading strategy.
